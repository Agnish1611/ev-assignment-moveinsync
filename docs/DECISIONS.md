| 2026-10-30 | D19: Construction stores deadhead duration as 0 seconds and stores the real deadhead distance and energy | The validator starts a trip activity at pickup minus the claimed duration, then requires the gap before that start to cover the same drive. A non-zero duration counts the drive twice and can push the activity before the horizon. Duration 0 makes the gap from the previous dropoff to the pickup the travel window, while the energy claims stay exact. Waits do not drain SoC, because the validator's energy walk does not apply idle power |
| 2026-10-30 | D20: A charge stop is inserted only after a direct append fails on energy or reserve. Its start SoC is the cab SoC minus the deadhead to the hub, and its target SoC is the energy the curve delivers in the booked duration. ALNS accepts a worse score on an integer cooling draw | The validator recomputes both SoC claims and requires the session length to be at least the curve duration for that target, so the planner stores the delivered target rather than a requested one. Trip deadhead duration stays 0 (D19). A trip that would fit with one more free plug is `CHARGER_CONGESTION`. Search starts from construction when `maxIterations` is positive, weights the existing destroy and repair operators, and accepts a worse score when a seeded draw is below a threshold that starts at 2000/10000 and is multiplied by 9/10 each iteration. The best score seen is returned. `maxIterations` 0 does not search |
| 2026-10-30 | D21: The flow re-timer runs once after construction and once on the ALNS incumbent. Trip assignments stay frozen. A session moves only when one contiguous block is feasible and strictly cheaper. The 60-second preemptive flow is a cost lower bound and is not published | Real sessions cannot be split, so the slot flow may spread energy across tariff fragments and hubs. That spread is a relaxation. The published plan keeps one block per session, books plugs on the half-open calendar, and reports the gap between the relaxation and the block cost. Destroy and repair still insert the earliest feasible stop |
| 2026-10-30 | D22: A battery or late-cab drift unlocks only that cab's failing trips inside the lock window. The ladder tries a same-cab charge, then the cab with the most slack, then one and two neighbour trips, and a safe patch if the change count would exceed 3 | The 22 percent reading cannot reach the next pickup after a charge, so that trip has to move. Locked trips on other cabs stay put. Neighbour repair is what frees a cab that is busy at the same instant. The safe patch unassigns only the trips that still do not fit. Tier 2 commits only while the plan id is the one the ladder started with |
| 2026-10-30 | D23: The backup lower bound is the maximum of intrinsically infeasible trips, peak overlap minus the cab count, and trips that do not fit in initial battery plus charger throughput. Trip energy for that pack is loaded distance plus a reserve leg, with deadhead omitted. The published cost lower bound remains the preemptive flow on the plan that was built | Overstating trip energy would claim backups a real plan can avoid. Omitting deadhead keeps the energy pack on the low side. Overlap treats a dropoff and the next pickup at the same instant as free. The flow relaxation is already a lower bound on the published session cost, so the report uses that figure next to the achieved cost |
| 2026-10-30 | D24: Named shifts are tight-battery, charger-scarce, peak-heavy, and the 2026-11-01 New York fallback, plus the fixed 300-cab evening fleet. The evening fleet ignores the seed. Drift injection sets one constant traffic factor, clamps sensor noise inside the pack, marks one SoC above capacity as an outlier, and leaves one cab silent | The evening snapshot is the published benchmark, so a seed must not move it. The other profiles stay small and change only by a 0-59 second offset and, for the tight profile, which cab starts low. Readings stay out of the planner input so an impossible SoC cannot be stored as a cab's starting energy |
| 2026-10-30 | D25: An unassigned trip is explained by offering it to every published cab route. If one extra operational plug at a hub makes a charge succeed, the reason is charger congestion and the evidence is that hub id plus the session window in UTC. Cab evidence stores arrival SoC and the planning floor in watt-hours. The per-cab timeline draws the empty drive from the distance provider and keeps the published assignment's deadhead duration at 0 | A float percentage would break the integer-energy rule. One extra plug is a capacity probe, so the synthetic plug is not written onto the plan. D19 still stores deadhead duration as 0 on the assignment; the timeline is the place that shows the real drive |
| 2026-10-30 | D26: A cancelled trip and a removed cab are taken out of the planner input before the ladder runs. A failed plug is copied as FAULTY. A reported SoC is accepted only when a session on that plug covers the observation and the reading equals that cab's input SoC. A cab strictly below the 15 percent floor is omitted and its trips are re-homed. A batch keeps the latest event for each kind and subject, then applies what remains in time order, with cab removal and charger failure ahead of battery drift and trip edits at the same timestamp | A schedule whose starting SoC is below the floor fails the battery check even with no activities, so the repaired plan leaves that cab out. The validator omits a trip only when that trip is gone from the input, so cancellation has to remove it there first. Events do not carry a time-to-breach, so the kind order is the stand-in when timestamps match |
| 2026-10-30 | D27: Tenant ids stay opaque text, and a plan becomes head only after the validator accepts it. | 2026-10-30 | D28: Events use a hand-written protobuf envelope (event id, tenant, producer, sequence, event time, schema version 1) whose field numbers live in ev/events.proto.
